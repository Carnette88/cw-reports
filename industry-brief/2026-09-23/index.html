Each one feeds the same conclusion for a trucking carrier — more shopping, firmer rates, and a risk pool in motion.

Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II
No developments since 2026-08-28 · 26 days
SCOTUS No. 24-1238; S.D. Ill. No. 19-cv-1300
On remand — S.D. Ill.
The Supreme Court held that the F4A does not preempt state negligent-hiring claims against freight brokers. The case itself is still awaiting trial in the Southern District of Illinois, but the doctrine it opened is already being applied downstream — most consequentially in the $604M Lipe v. Lupus Superior verdict against C.H. Robinson.
Why it matters. For two decades brokers and 3PLs leaned on F4A/FAAAA preemption to get negligent-hiring claims dismissed at the pleadings. The Supreme Court's May 2026 ruling closes that door: carrier-selection decisions now sit squarely inside the statute's safety exception, and plaintiffs can take a broker's choice of carrier to a jury under state law. That reshapes the risk map for anyone who places freight. Brokers and 3PLs face a new, uninsured-in-many-cases liability exposure, which pulls demand toward contingent auto liability, broker E&O, and vicarious-liability coverage, and raises the premium of documented carrier-vetting. It also strengthens the case for telematics-verified safety scoring at the point of carrier selection, which is adjacent to what DriveSmart already measures on the carrier side.
CW action. Treat this as a distribution and product talking point, not just legal news. Arm BD with a one-paragraph explainer for broker and 3PL prospects on how post-Montgomery liability changes their carrier-selection and insurance needs, and use it to open conversations with freight-adjacent partners. Flag to underwriting and product whether there is a contingent-liability or broker-facing coverage angle worth scoping while the market is reacting.
Next watch. District court scheduling on remand and the first substantive rulings on how the negligent-hiring standard is applied. The more material watch is now downstream: the backlog of negligent-selection cases that were parked pending the SCOTUS ruling and are moving again, broker and lessor vetting standards tightening, and any carrier appetite or rate-filing shifts on broker-facing coverages.

Lipe v. Lupus Superior (C.H. Robinson)
No developments since 2026-08-28 · 26 days
Dallas County, TX — district court; on appeal
$604M verdict — on appeal
A Dallas County jury returned roughly $604 million against broker C.H. Robinson, motor carrier Lupus Superior and the driver in a fatal 2021 crash. It is the first large-dollar test of the door the Supreme Court opened in Montgomery, and Robinson is appealing.
Why it matters. The jury assigned Robinson 23% of the fault but reached it through a borrowed-employee vicarious-liability theory, finding the driver was operating for the benefit of and under the control of the broker. Because the carrier is expected to be insolvent, joint-and-several liability exposes Robinson to far more than its 23% share — the precise scenario the industry feared. This is not a one-off: analysts describe a backlog of negligent-selection cases parked pending the SCOTUS ruling that is now moving forward, which makes it a repricing of liability across the entire freight-intermediary chain. The market read it that way immediately, with the Russell 3000 Trucking Index down more than 8% in its worst month since the tariff selloff, C.H. Robinson off 21%, and RXO and Landstar sliding in sympathy. For a trucking insurer the effect runs in our favor on demand and against us on severity: brokers and shippers will steer freight toward carriers who can document safety and carry adequate limits, which raises the value of a defensible telematics record, while the severity bar for every excess and umbrella layer moves up.
CW action. Use this as the opening line with broker, 3PL and agent prospects rather than price. The concrete ask brokers now have is documented carrier vetting, and DriveSmart's continuous underwriting record is the closest thing to an audit trail they can point a jury at. Have National Partners and Networks lead with the vetting angle, and flag to underwriting whether limit-adequacy conversations on the existing book need to move ahead of renewal.
Next watch. The appeal and any reduction of the award, whether the borrowed-employee theory survives appellate review, and how fast the parked negligent-selection backlog produces a second large verdict. Watch broker surety bond availability and any contingent-auto or broker E&O rate filings as the reinsurance market reacts.
