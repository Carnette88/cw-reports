Theme 02 · Loss Costs

Loss Costs Keep Ratcheting — Pricing Discipline Holds

Submission: MEDPricing: HIGHDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

Three separate threads this week all push the same way — the cost of a loss keeps climbing. A driver abandoned his rig after Nebraska troopers found $250,000 in stolen speakers moving on fraudulent paperwork, and a cargo-theft ring leader was sentenced over a $1.5 million spree, underscoring that theft severity is rising even where incident counts soften. On the liability side, the broker-transparency fight in Pink Cheetah v. TQL now hinges on whether a single FMCSA email can force disclosure of carrier-selection records — a live extension of the negligent-hiring exposure that the Supreme Court opened in Montgomery v. Caribe. And the escalating non-domiciled CDL fight goes to the heart of driver-qualification risk that underwriters price every day. Nothing here argues for softening rate; taken together, it reinforces why the commercial-auto premium environment stays hard.

CW Action

Lead with the DriveSmart underwriting story this week: continuous telematics-based driver-qualification screening is the direct answer to the non-domiciled CDL and negligent-hiring anxieties now surfacing in the trade press and the courts. Brief the BD team to reframe rising theft and liability severity not as a headwind but as the reason well-underwritten capacity commands its price, and to position Cover Whale's admitted and E&S mix as the structural advantage if minimums or severity keep climbing. Arm BD personnel with a short post-Montgomery talk track on why disciplined carrier selection protects everyone in the chain.
