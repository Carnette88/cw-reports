Theme 03 · Distribution Dynamics

Brokerage Consolidation Concentrates the Path to Small Carriers

Submission: MEDPricing: LOWDistribution: HIGH

Why It Matters

C.H. Robinson's move to acquire RXO combines two of the largest brokered-freight networks just as Optimus absorbs SmartHop's carrier-facing load and fleet platform and XPO extends its asset footprint with new service centers — a wave of consolidation concentrating control over how freight reaches small carriers. Fewer, larger intermediaries mean tougher carrier-qualification standards, higher insurance-limit demands, and more leverage over the operators that make up the bulk of the insured trucking base. This lands on top of the post-Montgomery broker-liability environment, in which brokers newly exposed to negligent-hiring claims have every incentive to push insurance requirements down onto carriers. For a trucking insurer, concentrated distribution reshapes demand, as broker insurance mandates increasingly dictate what coverage carriers must carry — which can lift limits and submission volume but also standardizes requirements in ways that favor carriers with clean, telematics-backed risk profiles. Distribution power is shifting, and the insurance requirement is becoming a competitive lever inside the freight supply chain.
