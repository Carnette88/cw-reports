Theme 02 · Risk & Loss Costs

Fraud and Cyber Threats Harden Into a Structural Loss Driver

Submission: MEDPricing: HIGHDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

A same-day cluster of events signals that fraud and cyber risk in freight have moved from nuisance to structural loss driver: the NMFTA stood up a shared cyber-threat portal, FMCSA is publicly recruiting carriers to help combat criminal infiltration, authorities charged a $105 million trucking-investment Ponzi scheme, a $300 million chip-smuggling case surfaced, and hackers reached an oil tanker's propulsion system. The throughline is that criminals increasingly exploit the industry's digital and identity seams, from FMCSA's new Motus registration system to broker-carrier onboarding. For an insurer this matters most on the loss side, where fictitious pickups, strategic cargo theft, and identity fraud drive both cargo and liability claims, and the sophistication is rising faster than many fleets' controls. It also reshapes underwriting, turning identity verification, telematics, and authority provenance into pricing variables rather than compliance checkboxes. Expect this to reinforce the commercial-auto hardening already running above 5% on rate.

CW Action

Position DriveSmart telematics and continuous underwriting as a fraud-and-theft control, not just a safety tool, and arm the BD team with a talk track connecting verified identity, vehicle provenance, and loss mitigation to premium stability. Tie the Carrier Access audit to this threat picture, since tightening onboarding verification is both a loss-cost play and a differentiator against carriers with looser controls. Watch whether the NMFTA portal and FMCSA Motus issues generate claims patterns CW should be pricing in now.
