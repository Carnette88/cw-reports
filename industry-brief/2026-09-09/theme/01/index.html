Washington Clears the Runway for Driverless Trucks

Submission: LOWPricing: MEDDistribution: LOW

Why It Matters

The Department of Transportation has published a national automated-vehicle strategy that directs FMCSA to revise the safety rules governing driverless commercial operation, and the trade press is reading it as a deliberate move to accelerate deployment rather than merely study it. The signals are converging fast: Kodiak reaffirmed a 2026 driverless long-haul launch, PlusAI is going public through a SPAC merger to fund scaling, and Waymo is expanding driver-out passenger service into new metros as the underlying stack matures. For a trucking insurance carrier this is a slow-moving but structural shift in the risk itself, because autonomous freight rewrites the loss model that decades of commercial-auto pricing were built on, moving liability toward manufacturers and technology stacks and away from the individual driver. The near-term submission and premium impact is limited, but the carriers that develop an underwriting point of view on mixed human-and-autonomous fleets first will own the conversation when those risks start hitting the market. The dynamic to watch is regulatory speed: if FMCSA rulemaking moves faster than expected, the appetite question arrives sooner than the industry is prepared for.

CW Action

Treat this as a strategy input, not a this-week scramble. Task the underwriting and product team with a short internal point of view on how DriveSmart telematics extends to fleets that mix human and autonomous units, so BD has a credible answer when large partners raise autonomy. Keep it on the roadmap radar rather than diverting Q2 surge resources.
