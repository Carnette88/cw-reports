Tariff Reshuffle Sweeps Volume to Motor Carriers — Capacity Is Already Responding

Submission: HIGHPricing: MEDDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

Tariff policy has restructured trade flows toward nearshoring and cross-border activity, and motor carriers' share of US import value has now reached 32 percent — the clearest evidence yet that the 2025-2026 supply-chain reshuffle is permanently advantaging trucking over ocean freight. The capacity side is responding in lockstep: Saia opened new terminals in Marysville, Washington and Edinburgh, Indiana to improve routing efficiency for the rerouted volume, and Overdrive's Trucker of the Month profile shows owner-operators scaling from one truck to three on the strength of the freight environment. Peterbilt's limited-edition 589 'Freedom 250' launch signals OEM confidence in fleet-replacement demand, the kind of decision OEMs only make when carriers are buying. The submission implication is direct — operators expanding capacity and rotating equipment will reshop coverage, and tariff-favored lanes will produce a meaningful uplift in new-business volume. This is the OTRI tightening signal Cover Whale built the Q2 surge thesis around, now showing up in the production data BD partners actually care about.

CW Action

Brief National Partners on the 32 percent motor-carrier import-value statistic this week and pivot retail and Network channel outreach toward cross-border and nearshoring lanes where freight is consolidating. Prioritize API partner activation for the operators expanding fleets in this environment — faster quote turnaround is the difference between capturing a scaling owner-operator and losing them to incumbent paper. Fold the Saia terminal-expansion signal into BD talk tracks as evidence that capacity is being added with intent, not desperation.
