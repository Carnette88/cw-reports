Theme 03 · Loss Severity

Loss Costs Are Climbing From Three Directions At Once

Submission: MEDPricing: HIGHDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

A cluster of claims-side signals this week all point to rising loss severity. An industry executive declared cargo theft the 'new normal' the same week a single California truck lost $963,000 in trading cards, New York arrested 16 people in a staged-insurance-crash ring, Arizona data suggested fraud schemes may be masking real losses even where reported theft is falling, and national data showed auto total-loss frequency climbing as more drivers absorb repairs themselves. Layer in the backdrop of nuclear verdicts — the FMCSA now pegs the median at $51 million against a federal minimum unchanged since 1985 — and the loss environment is hardening from theft, fraud, and litigation simultaneously. For a trucking carrier this is a direct pricing-power story: severity trends like these are what sustain rate adequacy and separate disciplined underwriters from those chasing growth into a worsening loss cost curve. It also sharpens the case for anything that reduces frequency and strengthens defensibility in court.

CW Action

Use this week's severity signals to defend rate discipline internally and to sharpen the DriveSmart value story externally — telematics evidence both reduces frequency and provides the exoneration data that blunts nuclear-verdict exposure, which is an increasingly concrete selling point as cargo theft and staged-crash fraud escalate. Brief the BD team on the fraud and cargo-theft narrative so they can position our underwriting and loss-control as protection rather than just price. Make sure any growth into fuel-stressed or distressed segments is paired with the severity data so we grow submissions without importing adverse loss experience.
