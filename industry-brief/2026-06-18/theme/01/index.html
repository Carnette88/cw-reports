Theme 01 · Broker Liability

Montgomery Hits The Field — C.H. Robinson Becomes The Live Test Case

Submission: HIGHPricing: MEDDistribution: HIGH

Why It Matters

The Supreme Court's Montgomery v. Caribe decision is no longer theoretical — it is now a Florida courtroom case with C.H. Robinson as the defendant in a 2025 fatal crash, the first real test of how broker-liability shielding plays out post-ruling. In parallel, both C.H. Robinson and J.B. Hunt are publicly signaling they view Montgomery as clarifying their dedicated and brokered models, which means the biggest 3PLs will lean harder into carrier-vetting and dedicated structures to capture the safe-harbor logic. For trucking insurance, this rewires distribution: brokers will accelerate scrutiny of which carriers they will tender freight to, push out the unvetted long tail, and concentrate freight with carriers who can document FMCSA compliance and telematics-based safety scoring. That is precisely the population Cover Whale already underwrites, and brokers needing defensible carrier files will increasingly want proof of continuous monitoring — not annual snapshots.

CW Action

Have BD weaponize Montgomery in talk tracks this week — DriveSmart continuous underwriting is exactly the carrier-vetting evidence brokers now need to defend a Florida-style suit. Push the Howden US Trucking Practice appointment and the National Partner channel to circulate a one-page broker brief on what Montgomery means for carrier selection, with Cover Whale positioned as the documentation-ready insured. Targeted outreach to API partners whose freight volume sits with mid-market brokers should land before quarter-end.
