Enforcement Is Pulling Capacity Out — The Submission Tailwind Is Here

Submission: HIGHPricing: HIGHDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

A rare convergence landed this week: FMCSA vowed to hold ELD-fraud violators 'fully accountable' and says its modernized Motus registration system has already barred thousands of bad actors from the highway, while enforcement-driven CDL revocations continue to thin the carrier ranks. At the same time the largest fleets are calling the turn — Werner's Derek Leathers expects accelerated capacity reductions and a multi-year upcycle, and Knight-Swift's leadership frames the regulatory crackdown as the engine of a durable recovery. The counterweight is visible too, with 221 small-carrier bankruptcies logged in 2026 as marginal operators fail. For a trucking insurer this is the cleanest leading indicator of rising submission flow: capacity that exits involuntarily forces the surviving and reforming operators to re-shop coverage, and a tightening market firms rates across the book. The dynamic validates the thesis behind a Q2 capacity-tightening surge rather than contradicting it.

CW Action

Treat this as the live confirmation of the OTRI capacity-tightening thesis and pull the Q2 surge messaging forward into current BD conversations. Arm the BD team with a concise capacity-exit talk track — enforcement is removing noncompliant competitors, and DriveSmart's continuous telematics underwriting lets Cover Whale say yes to the compliant, safety-minded operators those bad actors leave behind. Point FMCSA new-venture targeting at the reformed and re-authorizing carriers churning out of this cleanup, directly feeding the double-submissions OKR.
