The Broker Liability Reset Goes Operational — C.H. Robinson Pulls Carriers First

Submission: HIGHPricing: MEDDistribution: HIGH

Why It Matters

Three weeks after the Supreme Court stripped FAAAA preemption from negligent-carrier-selection claims in Montgomery v. Caribe, the largest 3PL in the country has moved first. C.H. Robinson tightened its carrier qualification standards this week and quietly removed carriers from active rosters, a signal that the rest of the brokerage industry will follow within the next few cycles. The simultaneous push to ban lease-purchase traps in the highway bill, paired with state and federal action on non-domiciled CDLs and undocumented operators, is closing every back door the lowest-tier carriers used to stay rated and rolling. For a trucking carrier writing in this market, that means a meaningful slice of the under-insured, under-vetted fleet population is about to get displaced from broker freight and forced to either upgrade their risk profile or exit. The carriers who upgrade need new insurance to do it, and the brokers themselves are about to demand cleaner cap-table evidence from the insureds they touch.

CW Action

Brief the BD team this week on a sharpened C.H. Robinson talking point: when 3PLs tighten carrier vetting, the carriers who survive are exactly the DriveSmart-monitored, telematics-verified risks Cover Whale is built for. Coordinate with Howden's US Trucking Practice on a one-pager that positions our continuous-underwriting model as the procurement-defensible insurance posture brokers can point to when they're asked, post-Caribe, why they kept a carrier on the roster. The displaced-carrier-shopping signal should be tracked in submission volume by the 25th of the month.
