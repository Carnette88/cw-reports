Policy Levers Move on Equipment Cost — FET Repeal Push and Adjacent Realignments

Submission: MEDPricing: MEDDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

A bipartisan Senate push reintroduced the FET repeal bill this week with formal ATA endorsement, putting the 12 percent federal excise tax on new heavy trucks and trailers back on the live agenda. Repeal would meaningfully shift the underlying capex math for fleet renewal and pull forward a wave of equipment purchases that have been deferred through the freight downcycle. Layer that with the Missouri speed-limit-increase push, AIG's turnaround positioning a leadership handoff, and Navium and Overhaul launching a brand-new appetite category for AI data server cargo, and the picture is one of structural pieces all moving at once. None of these are decided yet, but each one — passed or not — reshapes either the cost of operating a truck, the productivity of operating it, or the appetite for insuring what's on it. For an underwriter, that means the assumptions baked into renewal pricing two quarters from now are already drifting.

CW Action

Have the strategy team build a quick scenario brief on what FET repeal does to our new-venture funnel — newer equipment changes the loss-cost profile and likely improves our FMCSA new-venture conversion economics. Track the Senate bill's calendar weekly and prepare a position note for Howden if it advances to markup. Use Navium and Overhaul's AI-cargo launch as a reference point in the next API partner conversation about adjacent-appetite expansion through Cover Whale's continuous-underwriting platform.
