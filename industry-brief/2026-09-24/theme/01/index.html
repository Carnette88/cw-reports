Nuclear Verdicts Triple — Severity Is the Story Again

Submission: MEDPricing: HIGHDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

The severity side of commercial auto is deteriorating faster than frequency, and the evidence converged this week. Transport Topics reports the sum of large verdicts against the trucking and automotive sectors nearly tripled in 2025, to roughly 12 cases worth $3.4 billion versus $1.4 billion the year before, extending a hard market that has now run more than a decade. The dynamic is visible at ground level too: a single fatal Ohio Turnpike crash has already produced three separate lawsuits naming a long roster of defendants, an NTSB call to overhaul tolling infrastructure, and state lawmakers pressing California over the driver's licensure. For a carrier, this is the loss-cost backdrop that keeps rate adequacy elevated and rewards demonstrable risk selection. The plaintiff's bar is increasingly targeting anyone in the chain with a policy limit, which raises the premium every disciplined underwriter can defend charging.

CW Action

Arm the BD team with a sharpened severity talk track this week: nuclear-verdict inflation is not abstract, it is the reason DriveSmart's continuous, telematics-based underwriting is a pricing advantage rather than a compliance cost. Frame DriveSmart to prospects as documented, real-time risk-management evidence that both lowers a fleet's own verdict exposure and earns them a better rate than static annual raters can offer. Tie it to the loss-cost narrative in every renewal conversation where the incumbent is repricing blind.
