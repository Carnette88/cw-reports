Two Driver-Funnel Rulings in 24 Hours — Workforce Rules Tighten Mid-Cycle

Submission: MEDPricing: LOWDistribution: MED

Why It Matters

On June 1 the Supreme Court unanimously ruled that last-mile delivery drivers operating only intrastate can still qualify as engaged in interstate commerce for FAA Section 1 arbitration purposes — the fourth straight worker-side win on transportation classification and a meaningful expansion of who counts as part of the freight chain for legal purposes. The same day, the first formal exemption petition landed against the March FMCSA rule barring DACA recipients from obtaining or renewing CDLs — a rule that pulled a non-trivial slice of foreign-born drivers out of a labor pool already in its first shortage cycle in four years. These are two different mechanisms hitting the same constraint at the same time: the people legally allowed to move freight. For motor carrier underwriters this both tightens driver supply on the front end and adds litigation surface area around how brokers, shippers, and final-mile employers classify the workers in their chains.

CW Action

Underwriting and BD should jointly flag fleets in our book disproportionately exposed to the DACA-affected driver pool — the first formal challenge means more regulatory motion is likely, and the next 90 days will reward visible underwriting rigor. DriveSmart telematics is the strongest counter-signal we have against driver-supply-driven risk drift, so lean into it in renewal conversations on smaller fleets reshuffling their roster. For new-venture FMCSA targeting, prioritize operators documenting compliance discipline, not just miles.
