Litigation Watch

Standing tracker of the market-shaping cases we are following. Persists day to day; updated when a milestone lands.
Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II
SCOTUS No. 24-1238; S.D. Ill. No. 19-cv-1300
Remand pending — S.D. Ill.
The Supreme Court held that the F4A does not preempt state negligent-hiring claims against freight brokers. The case is now heading back to the federal district court in the Southern District of Illinois for trial.
Why it matters. For two decades brokers and 3PLs leaned on F4A/FAAAA preemption to get negligent-hiring claims dismissed at the pleadings. The Supreme Court's May 2026 ruling closes that door: carrier-selection decisions now sit squarely inside the statute's safety exception, and plaintiffs can take a broker's choice of carrier to a jury under state law. That reshapes the risk map for anyone who places freight. Brokers and 3PLs face a new, uninsured-in-many-cases liability exposure, which pulls demand toward contingent auto liability, broker E&O, and vicarious-liability coverage, and raises the premium of documented carrier-vetting. It also strengthens the case for telematics-verified safety scoring at the point of carrier selection, which is adjacent to what DriveSmart already measures on the carrier side.
CW action. Treat this as a distribution and product talking point, not just legal news. Arm BD with a one-paragraph explainer for broker and 3PL prospects on how post-Montgomery liability changes their carrier-selection and insurance needs, and use it to open conversations with freight-adjacent partners. Flag to underwriting and product whether there is a contingent-liability or broker-facing coverage angle worth scoping while the market is reacting.
Next watch. District court scheduling on remand and the first substantive rulings on how the negligent-hiring standard is applied. Watch for downstream state-court copycat filings and any carrier appetite or rate-filing shifts on broker-facing coverages.
